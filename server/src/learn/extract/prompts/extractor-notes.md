## The source: notes and files

The passage is part of a person's own notes or a document. It may be a few headings and bullets, a worked example, a definition list or a piece of prose. Part numbers in the header show where the passage sits in a longer file.

Record the substantive content of the passage: definitions, formulas, claims, findings and explanations. An idea that the notes state without a derivation is still worth recording as a proposition. Do not invent reasoning to connect ideas. Build a chain only where the notes themselves give the reasoning, or where the conclusion plainly follows from premises that the notes state.

Skip material that is not knowledge: navigation, front matter, to do items, plans for what to write later and remarks about the notes themselves. Where a note records an opinion or a decision, state it as a claim with its owner named, for example "the team chose X over Y because Z", and only when the passage gives the reason.

When the notes are terse, expand each statement until it can be understood alone, using only what the passage supports. Do not add outside knowledge.
