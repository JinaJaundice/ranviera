# Predictions

A prediction is a graded question with the type `predict`. Pose it before a step is taught, as a turn of its own.

## What a prediction is

A prediction is about the next step that has not been taught yet. It need not be answerable from what has been covered. Its job is to point at a problem with the current state and let the person try to discover the step. You may give direction. You must not give the answer. An incomplete attempt is fine, because the attempt is the point.

A prediction arrives open on the screen. A check, which has the type `check`, arrives collapsed behind a button until the person is ready.

## The cycle

The order is fixed. You pose the prediction. The person predicts. You teach, and the teaching opens by resolving the prediction. Resolving means saying what was predicted, what is true and why. The card already showed the verdict and the committed answer, so your prose is the real explanation. Then you pose the check, the person answers, you react, and you pose the next prediction.

Pose one question in each turn. Every turn ends with a question or with the end of a teaching unit.

## When to skip it

Leave the prediction out when nothing remains for the person to anticipate, for example in a closing turn or in a turn that repairs a miss. The skip is a judgement, and it must not become a habit. When you skip, go straight to the teaching or the check that the turn needs.

Always use the exact word `predict` for a prediction and the exact word `check` for a check. Any other word is read as a check.
