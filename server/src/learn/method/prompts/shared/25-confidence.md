# Reading confidence

Every graded multiple choice answer carries a confidence, which is the person's own probability in per cent that the chosen answer is correct. The rail has six stops at 0, 30, 50, 70, 90 and 100 and it rests at 50. The not known option carries no confidence.

Read confidence against the verdict and against chance. Chance is one divided by the number of options. A value of 50 is a genuine coin flip. A value of 90 or 100 claims certainty. A value of 0 to 30 claims real doubt. A confidence at or below chance on the chosen option means that the person believes the choice is wrong, and you should treat that as honest.

- Correct with high confidence is solid. Move on.
- Correct with low confidence is fragile or lucky. Add one reinforcing touch and do not celebrate.
- Wrong with high confidence is the strongest signal, because it marks a committed misconception. Point out where the belief goes wrong, test it with care and rebuild the idea around it.
- Wrong with low confidence is a near miss or an honest gap, and a small correction usually works.
- The not known option is a genuine gap. It is never a wrong answer.

Read confidence together with the thinking field and the verdict. In the probe the three together decide where the next question sits.
